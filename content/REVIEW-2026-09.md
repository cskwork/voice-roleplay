## Open questions for the human reviewers

Not changed, because each is a content decision or needs a native judgment rather than a clear error.

### English (native reviewer)

1. **cafe_order — tax included.** `facts.tax` says menu prices include tax. That is unusual in the US (tax is normally
   added at the register). It keeps totals simple for learners, and `expr_08` ("Does that price include tax?") teaches
   the question. Keep, or switch to "tax added at checkout" with a stated rate?
2. **cafe_order — tipping.** US cafes commonly show a tip prompt on the card reader. The scenario never mentions tips.
   Add a fact (for example "a tip screen appears; tipping is optional") so learners practice it, or leave it out?
3. **cafe_order — "menu_iced_tea" is "Black tea".** The fact key says iced tea, the value says black tea; `sizes` says
   every drink can be hot or iced, so "a large iced tea" (`expr_01`) still works. Confirm the LLM handles "iced tea"
   naturally, or rename the value to "Iced or hot black tea".
4. **cafe_order — `read_02` "Could you make it a little less sweet?"** No sweetness option exists in the facts (only
   syrups). Fine as a standalone reading sentence; confirm.
5. **hotel_checkin — total with tax.** The rate is "$189.00 per night before tax" but no tax rate is given. If a guest
   asks for the total, the LLM can only say "$567.00 before tax" or it may invent a tax figure. Add a tax rate (for
   example a stated percentage and the resulting total), or add an `allowed_flow` line: "If asked about tax, say the
   exact amount will be on the final bill"?
6. **hotel_checkin — photo ID.** US front desks normally ask for a photo ID along with the card. Add to `allowed_flow`?
7. **directions — walking time.** Three city blocks at "about 10 minutes" implies long blocks (about 250 m each).
   Plausible, but many US downtown blocks take 1–2 minutes each. Keep, or change to "about 5 minutes"?
8. **directions — `hint_03` "…turn left onto Oak Street, right?"** Natural tag question, but "left … right?" can confuse
   beginners. Keep (realistic), or use "…, is that correct?" in the easy hint?
9. **job_interview — opening line.** "Thanks for coming in today. I'm Sarah, …, so why don't you start by telling me a
   little about yourself?" joins two ideas with ", so"; natural in speech. Kept to respect the 1–2 sentence rule.
10. **job_interview — team size.** "A four-person growth marketing team": does four include the new hire? Only matters
    if a learner asks.
11. **job_interview — `expr_04` "raised our open rate by fifteen percent".** Relative percent vs percentage points is
    ambiguous; fine for conversation. Confirm.
