# CTC GOP (PA-2) and its calibration (PA-3) on speechocean762 — 2026-09-28

> **Read this first.** Every speaker in speechocean762 is a Mandarin-L1 learner (half of them children). None of these
> numbers say anything about Korean learners. The Korean-learner set (PA-0) does not exist, so the adoption criteria
> (PROTOCOL §12.3: phone r ≥ 0.55 and word r ≥ 0.5 on that set) **cannot be checked**. Bands therefore stay **off by
> default** (`VR_PRON_EXPERIMENTAL` unset). This system is also **below the published GOPT baseline** at every level, on
> the same corpus.

## What was measured

- **System:** the pronunciation worker's `/assess` pipeline (`workers/pronunciation`): Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B word
  timings (rev `c7cbfc2`) → facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft phone posteriors (rev `ae45363`, MPS fp32) →
  segmentation-free CTC GOP per expected unit over a window of previous + current + next word (± 200 ms). Unit table `units-2`.
  The method is in `workers/pronunciation/README.md`.
- **Expected phones:** the corpus's own canonical ARPAbet (`gop_ctc`, `gop_ctc_cal`), so each score lines up with a human
  phone score. The worker uses CMUdict instead; `gop_ctc_cal_cmudict` measures that path at word and sentence level.
- **Calibration** (`workers/pronunciation/calibration/so762-ctcgop-2026-09-28.json`): phone model (GOP, posterior and
  a per-unit bias → human phone accuracy 0–2) and word model (mean and min of the phone predictions → word accuracy 0–10),
  both ridge least squares. The two band thresholds maximise 3-class macro-F1 on train. A benchmark-only sentence model
  (mean and min of the word predictions) exists only for comparison with published results; the worker has no sentence score.
- **Protocol:** coefficients, thresholds and feature choices come from **train only**. Feature variants were compared on a
  speaker-disjoint 2-fold split inside train. Test (2,500 utterances, 125 speakers, 15,967 words, 47,369 phones) was
  used for this report only. The metric code is `run_eval.evaluate` in every case. `fit_calibration.py`'s test numbers were
  reproduced exactly by an end-to-end `run_eval.py --system gop_ctc_cal` run that loads the calibration file through the
  worker's own `pron_worker.calibration`.
- **Machine:** Apple M3 Pro 36 GB, macOS 26.6.2, while other workloads were running (one process used ~9 CPU cores).

## Correlation with human scores (Pearson; Spearman in brackets)

| Test split | Phone accuracy | Word accuracy | Utterance accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw GOP (`gop_ctc`; word = mean unit GOP) | 0.468 (0.359) | 0.422 (0.358) | — |
| Calibrated (`gop_ctc_cal`) | **0.480** (0.386) | **0.463** (0.385) | **0.616** (0.571) |
| Calibrated, CMUdict phones as in the worker (`gop_ctc_cal_cmudict`) | — (no 1:1 phone mapping) | 0.472 (0.392), n = 15,950 | 0.623 (0.601) |
| Published: GOPT (Gong et al., ICASSP 2022) | 0.612 | 0.549 | 0.742 |

Train split, for reference (in-sample for the calibrated row): raw 0.512 / 0.496; calibrated 0.526 / 0.533 / 0.699.
The 2-fold speaker-disjoint estimate inside train was 0.523 (phone) / 0.527 (word). Richer phone features (squared GOP,
GOP indicator steps) added about 0.01 there and were not adopted.

- **The gap to GOPT is large:** −0.13 phone, −0.09 word, −0.13 utterance. GOPT feeds 84-dimensional GOP features per
  phone from a Kaldi Librispeech model into a transformer. Here one scalar GOP per unit goes into a linear map.
- **Accent of the lexicon.** The corpus lexicon is partly non-rhotic ("for" = F AO0) and partly British ("ask", "last" with
  AA). The model hears American forms. Rhotic/non-rhotic variants are accepted (unit table `units-2`); the bath vowel is not.
  For 22 % of the in-lexicon words of both splits, none of the CMUdict pronunciations gives the same units as the corpus lexicon. CMUdict lacks 17 of the 15,967 test words
  (OOV = no output) and 39 of 31,816 words in both splits.
- **Age groups (test, calibrated):** adults word r 0.488, phone r 0.481; children word r 0.412, phone r 0.476.
- **Per speaker (test, calibrated, word r):** median 0.254, quartiles 0.177–0.396, range −0.073 to 0.808 (123 speakers
  with a defined r). Correlation within one speaker is much lower than over the pooled test set.

## Bands (only with `VR_PRON_EXPERIMENTAL=1`)

Thresholds on predicted word accuracy, chosen on train: `practice` < 7.9 ≤ `check` < 8.4 ≤ `good` (train macro-F1 0.512).
Human classes follow the corpus rubric: practice = word accuracy 0–3, check = 4–6, good = 7–10.

Test confusion (rows = human class, columns = predicted band):

| human \ band | good | check | practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| good (7–10) | 13,005 | 816 | 849 |
| check (4–6) | 220 | 86 | 133 |
| practice (0–3) | 222 | 159 | 477 |

"Flagged" = band is not `good`. The table compares this with the v0.1 reading diff (`asr_diff-test.md`, same test words):

| Test split, human "mispronounced" = word accuracy ≤ 6 (1,297 words) | Miss rate | Precision | Flagged among words scored 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| v0.1 reading diff (`asr_diff`) | 39.8 % | 42.9 % | 6.6 % |
| Calibrated bands (`gop_ctc_cal`) | 34.1 % | 33.9 % | 10.7 % |
| Calibrated bands, CMUdict phones (`gop_ctc_cal_cmudict`) | 36.1 % | 36.3 % | 9.3 % |

- The bands miss fewer mispronounced words than the ASR diff, but they flag more words that the experts scored 10.
  On test, 2,520 words were flagged and 1,665 of them (66 %) had human accuracy ≥ 7.
- Severe errors (accuracy ≤ 3): miss rate 25.9 %; any phone < 0.5: miss rate 22.5 % (`gop_ctc_cal`).
- Adults miss rate 31.0 % (precision 39.8 %), children 41.2 % (precision 24.4 %).
- Per speaker (56 speakers with ≥ 5 mispronounced words): miss rate median 39.5 %, range 0–100 %.
- On train the same thresholds gave miss rate 27.5 %, precision 42.1 %, 10.2 % of words scored 10 flagged.

## Sanity checks on synthetic speech (not accuracy)

The worker's integration tests pair correct macOS `say` speech with a deliberately wrong reference word. The first unit's GOP
dropped from −0.001 to −11.4 (milk→silk), −0.004 to −9.4 (like→bike), −0.18 to −9.6 (oat→eat) and −0.009 to −6.7
(pay→bay), and the unit actually spoken was among the heard candidates each time. With the flag on, "silk" is not
`good`. There was one false alarm: 1 of 9 words of a correct reading ("cappuccino": CMUdict's unstressed `uː` was spoken
as a reduced vowel) was banded `practice`.

## Not done / open

- **No Korean-learner measurement**: the PA-0 set does not exist. The adoption criteria are not met and cannot be checked.
- The PA-2 acceptance criterion "한국어 악센트 별도" (a separate Korean-accent report) cannot be produced for the same reason.
- Phone-level timing accuracy was not measured against hand labels. Heard-candidate accuracy (does "X instead of Y" match
  what experts transcribed in `mispronunciations`?) was not evaluated either.
- Possible next steps, none tried here: GOPT-style multi-dimensional features (the full candidate posterior vector per
  unit), accepting reduced vowels for unstressed UW0/IY0, and expanding digits before the lexicon lookup.
